# CRSS — Recorded Demo Playbook

Everything you need to record a ~2:30–3:00 demo video of CRSS answering Braxton's question,
and post it so RA & compliance teams (medical devices + AI) actually watch it. This is a
reusable asset — one good recording you send to everyone, drop in DMs, and pin to your profile.

Audience: RA officers, compliance leads, consultants, notified-body people. Time-poor,
skeptical, allergic to hype. **Sincerity beats polish. The tool does the talking.**

---

## 0. Mindset (read before you record)

- **You are not the regulatory expert — they are.** Your credibility = the tool + your
  honesty. Lean into *"I built this; you live this; that's why I want your eyes on it."*
- **No hype, ever.** One overclaim loses this crowd. "Here's what it can't do yet" earns more
  trust than any superlative.
- **The 1:40 processing time is a FEATURE for this audience.** An *instant* legal answer is a
  red flag (that's a model hallucinating). "Slow because it checks itself first" is the sell.
- **Ship the rough first cut.** The enemy isn't a mediocre video — it's the perfect one you
  never post. Done > perfect. It gets easier by your third one.

## 2. The full script (voiceover)

Word-for-word "SAY" — adapt to your voice. **Confirm in a dry run that CRSS actually surfaces
the "safety component" point and the citations. The real screen is the boss — if it words
things differently, match your narration to it.**

### Beat 1 — The hook (0:00–0:18)
- **SCREEN:** The question on screen as plain text (don't name who asked it).
- **SAY:** *"A question came up in an EU compliance community, and it looks simple until you
  try to answer it: a company builds an LLM into a medical device — but for something
  non-medical, not diagnosis or treatment. Is that LLM a high-risk AI system under the AI
  Act, or not?"*

### Beat 2 — The tension (0:18–0:30)
- **SCREEN:** Keep the question up; highlight the two halves.
- **SAY:** *"You can argue it both ways. The device sits inside the AI Act's Annex I — so
  maybe high-risk. But the LLM isn't doing anything medical — so maybe not. Which is it?"*

### Beat 3 — Ask it (0:30–0:40)
- **SCREEN:** Paste the question into CRSS, hit enter.
- **SAY:** *"So let me just ask the system I've been building, and watch what it does."*

### Beat 4 — The wait = how it works (0:40–2:05) — your best segment
- **SCREEN:** The draft container where you can see it working. Point at it.
- **SAY:** *"Now — it's not instant, and that's the point. While it works, let me show you
  what's actually happening.*
  *Right here, it's writing a first draft — but it won't show you that draft. It takes its own
  first attempt and does a second pass over it, re-checking every claim against the actual
  text of the regulations it pulled in.*
  *That's deliberate. An instant legal answer is a red flag — that's a model autocompleting
  from memory, and that's exactly how you get confident, fluent, completely invented
  citations. This does the opposite: it's slower because it's verifying itself before it shows
  you a single word.*
  *Underneath, it's not treating the regulations as a flat pile of text.* **[OPTIONAL: cut to
  graph — see §4]** *These are the actual provisions, connected by their cross-references —
  the AI Act here, wired to the MDR here. That web is what lets it reason about how the rules
  interact, not just look them up one at a time.*
  *And on this second pass, if it wrote a quote that isn't actually in the source, it catches
  it and fixes or flags it. So what's about to appear isn't a first guess — it's a checked
  answer. Here it comes."*

### Beat 5 — The answer, the "aha" (2:05–2:40)
- **SCREEN:** Final answer. Slowly scroll/highlight: (1) safety-component point, (2) a
  citation, (3) the GPAI note.
- **SAY:** *"And here's why the wait earns its keep. It doesn't get stuck on 'is the purpose
  medical' — it goes to the test that actually decides this: is the LLM a* safety component
  *of the device? That's Article 6 — and the definition it turns on is right here in Article
  3. Every claim links back to the provision it rests on. And it flags what most people miss:
  even if it's not high-risk as a device component, an LLM is usually a general-purpose AI
  model — with its own obligations."*

### Beat 6 — Honest frame + ask (2:40–2:57)
- **SCREEN:** Rest on the citations / any uncertainty flag.
- **SAY:** *"This isn't legal advice, and it doesn't pretend to be — every quote is verified
  against the real text, and it still needs an expert to sign off. I built it on my own, for
  regulatory and compliance teams. If that's your world, I'd genuinely value your eyes on it —
  tell me where it's wrong."*

## 3. Recording workflow (record-friendly for a first-timer)

Do it in two layers — don't talk and drive the demo at the same time:

1. **Record the screen silently first.** Just paste the question, let it run the full 1:40,
   final answer appears. Zero pressure, no talking. (Grab the optional graph glimpse as a
   separate 10-second screen recording, §4.)
2. **Then record the voiceover over it.** In iMovie, drop the clip on the timeline, press
   **V**, and record narration straight onto it while it plays, reading the script. Redo the
   voiceover as many times as you want without ever re-recording the screen. This is where
   the NT-1A shines.

**Timing the wait:** your "how it works" narration fills the 1:40. If your voice finishes
before the answer appears, select the silent gap and bump it to 2× (iMovie speedometer). If
it appears too soon, nudge the clip. Easy.

**NT-1A tips:** quiet room; pop filter *or* sit ~15–20 cm back and speak slightly *past* it
(not into it) to avoid popped "p"s; watch levels so peaks don't clip (~−12 to −6 dB).

**Captions:** most people watch muted — add them. LinkedIn can auto-generate on upload; don't
let captions block you from posting. Skip the webcam for v1 (just screen + voice).

## 4. The optional graph glimpse (5–8s)

Worth it — it's your best visual proof that CRSS reasons over *interacting* regulations (your
differentiator vs. flat "AI-for-compliance" tools). Rules:
- **Frame it as regulations, not a database.** Never say "Neo4j / nodes / Cypher." Say "the
  actual regulations, connected by their cross-references."
- **Small, legible subgraph — not a hairball.** Pre-stage a clean view: node captions set to
  `display_ref`, colored by label, a handful of AI Act nodes wired to MDR nodes.
- **Pre-stage it; never run a live query on camera.** Grab it as a separate 10s recording and
  splice it into the wait segment at the marked point.
- 5–8 seconds, then straight back. The moment it becomes about *your tech* instead of *their
  regulations*, you've lost them.

## 7. After you post — turn views into conversations

The recording is the top of the funnel; the *replies* are where pilots start.
- **Answer every comment** thoughtfully and without defensiveness.
- **When someone engages, DM them** a genuine follow-up: *"Thanks for the thoughtful comment —
  since you clearly live this, would you be up for putting a real question to it and telling
  me where it breaks?"*
- Use the honest answers below for comments/DMs:

**"Isn't this just ChatGPT?"** → *"A general chatbot answers from memory and invents
citations. CRSS answers only from the legal text it retrieved, checks its own quotes against
the source, and shows where each claim comes from — you saw it flag its own uncertainty. But
tell me if it holds up under real questions."*

**"How do I know it's accurate?"** → *"Every claim is traceable, so you can verify it in
seconds; and it checks its own quotes and flags what it can't ground. It's decision support —
you still confirm. That's what a pilot is for: finding where it's wrong."*

**"Is this legal advice / liability?"** → *"No — it's a research and drafting aid. It doesn't
replace your regulatory or legal judgment, and it's not a compliance guarantee."*

**"What about our data?"** → *[Answer truthfully for your setup. Safe honest version:]* *"It
runs on public legal sources and doesn't need your confidential data to answer regulatory
questions. For a pilot we'd agree exactly what, if anything, is shared."*

**"Which regs does it cover?"** → *"AI Act, MDR, IVDR, GDPR, the CIR 2026/977, plus AI Act and
MDCG guidance. Growing."*

**When you don't know:** *"Honestly, that's more your world than mine — how do you handle that
today?"* Turns a gap into discovery. Your strongest move, not a weakness.

## 8. Substance cheat-sheet (so you can speak with confidence)

Treat this as your *working understanding* — the legal text, CRSS, and a real expert are the
authorities. Enough to frame the problem and understand your own demo.

### The AI Act ↔ MDR connection, in plain terms
- Medical devices → **MDR (2017/745)**; IVDs → **IVDR (2017/746)**. Both are listed in the AI
  Act's **Annex I**.
- The AI Act has **two doors into "high-risk":**
  - **Door 1 — Article 6(1) (product route):** your AI is high-risk if it's a *safety
    component of*, or *is*, a product covered by Annex I (a medical device) **and** that
    product needs **third-party conformity assessment** (a notified body). Most Class IIa+
    devices / most IVDs qualify.
  - **Door 2 — Article 6(2) + Annex III (use-case route):** stand-alone AI in listed use cases
    (biometrics, employment, …), with a limited self-assessment carve-out (Art 6(3)).
- **"Safety component" (Article 3):** a part whose *safety function*, or whose *failure*,
  affects health/safety. **This is the hinge of Braxton's question.**
- **Article 43:** where both apply, conformity assessment runs through the **MDR procedure**
  with AI-Act requirements folded in — one assessment, not two.
- **GPAI models (Chapter V):** an **LLM is usually a GPAI model**, with its *own* obligations
  *regardless* of the high-risk question.
- **Timelines (after the Digital Omnibus, Reg 2026/1744):** high-risk under Annex III →
  **2 Dec 2027**; high-risk under Annex I (device AI) → **2 Aug 2028**. *(That these just moved
  is itself a perfect example of the problem CRSS solves.)*

### Braxton's question, explained (understand your own demo)
- **The trap:** arguing whether the LLM's *purpose* is "medical." Wrong test.
- **The real test:** is the LLM a **safety component** (Art 6(1) + Art 3)? If its function is
  genuinely non-safety and its failure wouldn't endanger health/safety, it's likely **not**
  high-risk *via the device route*.
- **Don't stop there:** check the other door (Annex III), and remember the LLM is likely a
  **GPAI model** with its own obligations. "Not high-risk as a device component" ≠ "no
  obligations."
- **The nuance that impresses experts:** it's fact-specific — how the LLM is integrated can
  pull it into "safety component" territory even if its purpose sounds non-medical. So the
  honest answer is "here's the decisive test and the provisions — an expert confirms."
- **Why it's a great demo:** the naive answers are *both* wrong; CRSS surfaces the *right
  question*.

### Mini-glossary
- **High-risk AI system** — the AI Act tier with the heavy obligations.
- **Annex I vs III** — Annex I = existing product laws (incl. MDR/IVDR); Annex III = stand-alone
  high-risk use cases.
- **Conformity assessment / notified body** — the third-party check before CE marking; the AI
  Act plugs into it for device AI.
- **Intended purpose** — the MDR concept that fixes what a device is *for*.
- **GPAI** — general-purpose AI model (e.g. an LLM); has its own AI Act obligations.

## 9. Do's & don'ts

**Do** — open on the question (3-second rule) · make the wait a feature · show the citations ·
say "decision support, not legal advice" · invite experts to correct you · keep it under 3:00 ·
ship the rough cut.

**Don't** — hype or overclaim · out-expert the experts · hide the limitations · say
"Neo4j/nodes/Cypher" · demo an unverified answer · let it run past 3 minutes.

---

## 10. The through-line (if you remember nothing else)

**The question → watch it think (carefully, not fast) → the citations → "you're the expert,
tell me where it's wrong."** Lead with their problem, show it work, be honest about the limits,
hand the expertise back to them. That's the whole video.
